# The Industrialization of Software Development

Before industrialization, everything required a manual process to be built. If you wanted a table you needed to know the materials, the tools, and the process to build it, or pay someone to do it for you.

With the industrial revolution, we automated the process of building most things, you can now go to a store and buy a generic table that is mass-produced. This has made it easier and more efficient to acquire the things we need, but it has also led to a loss of craftsmanship and individuality in the products we use.

We can still find artisans who create unique, handcrafted tables, but they are often more expensive and less accessible than mass-produced options, they also tend to be of higher quality and have a longer lifespan than mass-produced tables.

The rise of AI agents like Codex, OpenCode, Claude, etc. is leading the software industry to a similar industrialization process.

These AI agents are automating a lot of repetitive tasks, and in many cases a lot of software can be generated by these agents with minimal human input. This is huge for the industry, as it not only allows for faster development, but also allows for more people to create software without needing to have a deep understanding of programming.

However, just like with the industrialization of physical products, the quality of this mass-produced software is not the best, it's ok for many cases of course, the same way not everyone needs a handcrafted table, not everyone needs a handcrafted software.

Many companies that works on similar CRUD applications for multiple clients will be replaced by AI agents that can generate the software, or will adopt it internally to speed up their development process.

I don't think this will lead to a loss of jobs in the software industry, not only more complex and innovative products will stillr require a human behind them to design and engineer them, a lot of companies will still need a human, a software engineer, to manage and guide their AI agents.

I can imagine a future where a product company can have a small team of senior software engineers, the architects of the software, translating the product vision and requirements into a software design, sometimes manually writting code for specific parts of the software.

Imagine a senior frontend engineer that manually work on the design system of the company, then AI agents can use those components to generate the different screens of the application. Or a backend engineer that manually work on the database design and the API design, then AI agents can generate the different endpoints of the API.

The same way a car can be manufactured in a factory, but still require a team of engineers to design and oversee the production process, software can be generated by AI agents, but still require a team of software engineers to design and oversee the development process.

## The Future of the Junior Software Engineer

Right now, many companies expect junior developers to be able to write code, and to have a good understanding of programming concepts, but in the future, I think this will change.

First of all, I think the demand for juniors will actually increase, as there's more and more code being produced, you need more people to review it and test it, and this is a perfect role for juniors.

A recently graduated software engineer can join a senior engineer in the process of designing the software, and then be responsible for reviewing the code generated by the AI agents, testing it, and making sure it meets the requirements and quality standards.

Juniors will take the role of a QA engineer, but instead of only testing the software, they will review the code produced by the agent, and make sure it follows the design and requirements. Eventually as they gain more experience, they can be tasked to lead the design of specific features, becoming a senior engineer in the process.

## QA Engineers

To be honest, I never understood why being a QA engineer was a separate role from being a software engineer. When I worked with a QA engineer, they where of great help to catch bugs and regressions, of course, but I felt that they should strive to become software engineers, and not just be testers.

That's why I think juniors will eventually take this role, the QA engineer will become a junior software engineer, and not a separate career path.

This matches how other industries like game development works too, where a new graduate can start as a QA tester, and then move up to become a game designer or a game developer. You don't expect someone with 10 years of experience to still be a QA test, you expect them to have moved up to a more senior role, and the same should apply to software engineering.

## The Mid-Level Software Engineer

This is the only role that I think will be completely replaced by AI agents, the mid-level software engineer is the one that is responsible for writing most of the code, and this is the part that can be easily automated by AI agents.

In most companies, a mid-level software engineer is the one that receives a task in some ticker management system, and then is responsible for writing the code to implement that task, and then submit it for review. This is a very repetitive process, and it's the perfect candidate for automation by AI agents.

I truly believe there's no future for this kind of blue-collar software engineer, AI agents do this and do it better, faster, and probably cheaper than a human. Every mid-level needs to level up now, and start learning how to design software, how to oversee the development process, and how to train junior engineers, this is the only way to stay relevant in the future of software development.

## The Senior Software Engineer

I already mentioned some of my thoughts on the role of the senior software engineer, but I want to expand on it a bit more.

Right now, many Senior Software Engineers are still very much involved in writing code, in some companies being a senior is just a matter of years of experience, and not necessarily a matter of skills or responsibilities.

This needs to change, if I were to lead a software company, I would prefer to hire a few senior engineers that are good at code, but are better at designing software, and by "designing" I don't mean just the visual part, but the overall architecture and structure of the software, how the different components interact with each other, how the data flows through the system, etc.

A good senior frontend engineer should know the ins and outs of how design systems work, accessibility, performance, etc. A good senior backend engineer should know how to design scalable and maintainable APIs, how to design databases, how to handle security, etc.

Then they can translate business requirements into software design documents, pass those to the AI agents, and let the agent generate the code, occasionally writing some code manually for specific parts of the software that the human consider is important to get right, or maybe not writing the code by hand, but carefully guiding the agent in the whole process.

Seniors should also be mentors for junior engineers, by reducing the amount of engineers a team may need to one senior + AI agents, the role of the senior as an actual mentor of a junior that just graduated from college becomes more important and possible.

## The Importance of College Education

Many great software engineer are self-taught, and they don't have a college degree, but I think that in the future, having a college degree will become more important for software engineers. You don't want to hire as a junior someone who doesn't know the basics of programming, system design, algorithms, data structures, etc. and a college education can provide that foundation.

This is similar to what happens in other industries, for example, you may hire a self-taught electrician to do simple tasks on your house, but if you need to work on a energy plant, you would want to hire an engineer with a proper education, not just a self-taught electrician.

The same can be applied to software, a self-taught developer may be able to work on simple projects, using AI yes, but still simpler, thing a CRUD application used internally by a small company. But for truly complex and innovative products, you would want to hire a junior engineer with a proper education, that has a solid foundation from college.
