I have used eleven notes apps since university. Every one of them kept my words perfectly. Not one of them kept the reason I wrote them down, and that is what I go back for.

## A note is a moment

When I reread an old note, the questions I ask are rarely about its text. _What was I working on? What had I just read? What did this replace?_ The answers lived in the moment around the note, and the app threw that moment away the second I closed the window.

The tools that have helped me think keep some of it. A backlink records that one idea led to another. A daily page records what else was on my mind. A "created from" link records the article or meeting a note came out of. None of these are features you notice on day one; all of them pay off in year three.

## Why local-first matters here

Context is personal and it accumulates slowly, so it has to outlive the app. That is the strongest argument I know for local-first notes: plain files or a database I own, synced between my devices, readable by the next tool when this one is gone. A note whose links live on someone else's server is a note whose _why_ has an expiry date.

> [!NOTE]
> I keep backlinks as data, not as a feature of one app: every link is a row of source, target and date. Moving tools means exporting a table, not re-linking a thousand pages.

## What I want from the next one

Keep my words, sure. But also keep where I was when I wrote them, and show me the trail back. Digital gardens, wikis and outliners all get part of the way. The rest is sync engines that treat links and timestamps as first-class data, which is most of what this blog is about.
